### 📖 About Page

**Founded:** March 2020

**Location:** Indrawati Rural Municipality — Sipapokhare Ward 7 and Bhimtar Ward 12

**Core Values:**
1. **Community Ownership:** Programs are community-initiated, designed, and implemented.
2. **Intersectional Justice:** We address overlapping oppressions — caste, gender, and poverty — not as separate issues but as systems that must be dismantled together.
3. **Ethical Partnerships:** We collaborate with institutions and funders who value community voice, thoughtful approach, and long-term impact.

**Goals:**
1. **Build Women's Leadership** — Create sustained opportunities for Dalit and Janajati women to lead — as entrepreneurs, mentors, and organizers within their own communities.
2. **Strengthen Local Livelihoods** — Facilitate skills-based training and savings groups that support women in generating income and achieving economic independence.
3. **Advance Community Education** — Support access to quality education for children, while promoting legal awareness, digital literacy, and reproductive health knowledge for women.
4. **Foster Peer-Led Change** — Train local women as peer mentors and facilitators who carry forward the work — ensuring sustainability through shared leadership.
5. **Disrupt Structural Inequity** — Challenge caste- and gender-based oppression through grassroots advocacy, storytelling, and ethical partnerships rooted in justice.

**About Text:** "At Heart of Nepal, we believe in harnessing the collective strengths of community and fostering an ecosystem of knowledge, resources, and agency. Our holistic model catalyzes lasting social transformation from within, challenging long-standing prejudices and regressive norms that have historically subjugated the community."

### 📍 Geographical Area of Intervention Page

#### Sindupalchowk District
- **Population and Demographics:**
  - Total Population: 287,798
  - Significant Ethnic Groups: Tamang (24.25%), Newar (11.11%), Dalits (various sub-groups)
- **2015 Earthquake Impact:**
  - Over 90% of houses destroyed
  - 3,550 fatalities
- **Human Trafficking:**
  - One of the most trafficking-prone districts
  - Increasing complexity in trafficking purposes
  - 60% of trafficked girls are from the Janajati population
- **Economic Activities:**
  - Primary Occupation: Agriculture
  - Agricultural Land: 73,710 hectares (out of 254,200 hectares total)
  - Low fertility and yield due to hilly terrain
  - Supplementary Income: Seasonal labor in tourism and small-to-medium enterprises
- **Post-Earthquake Vulnerability:**
  - Increased vulnerability of female-headed households
  - Focus on small-scale agriculture and livestock for family consumption

#### Indrawati Rural Municipality
> According to the 2011 population census, Indrawati Rural Municipality has a total population of 28,517 across 6,211 households. This municipality has a high proportion of Majhi, Danuwar, and Tamang communities. Despite having 40 primary schools and 12 secondary schools, less than half of the Dalit and Janajati children are enrolled in school.
>
> The 2011 Census also revealed that fewer than 100 dwellings in the municipality were constructed with brick and cement. The 2015 earthquake caused extensive damage, which led to significant reconstruction efforts by the Government of Nepal and various international organizations.
>
> Currently, there are no NGOs operating in the targeted wards of the municipality. Previously, two organizations, Friends of Sankhu and Child Nepal, were active but have phased out due to a lack of funds.

### 📖 Stories From The Ground Page

#### Story 1 — Empowering Change: Lalita Gurung's Journey from Housewife to Successful Entrepreneur
- **Group:** "Paurakhi Mother's Group" of "Pariwartansheel Self Help Group"
- **Narrative:**
  > I am Lalita Gurung (Majhi), a 38-year-old resident of Ward No. 12 in Indrawati Rural Municipality. Coming from a poor Janajati farming family, I married a Majhi gentleman from Dhotar village, and together we have two growing children. Despite completing my 10+2 education, I was unable to secure a job. Lacking the financial resources for self-employment, I found myself confined to the home, struggling to afford even the modest school fees for my children.
  >
  > Everything changed when HEART of Nepal (HoN) began a Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) survey in our ward before launching their project. I learned about various community-based programs HoN planned to implement, such as bridge courses for dropout children, school enrollment campaigns, scholarship support, tuition classes, and Women Self Help Groups (WSHG).
  >
  > Eager to improve my circumstances, I joined a WSHG formed by HoN in Dhotar village. Last year, I participated in several training sessions on saving, credit management, financial literacy, and leadership development organized by HoN. During this time, HoN staff reorganized the WSHG into different thematic groups. Choosing to join the Poultry Group, I received a three-day training on poultry rearing, feeding, disease management, vaccination, and overall health care and management of poultry.
  >
  > Following the detailed instructions and guidance provided during the training, I meticulously applied the knowledge and techniques I had learned. With the commencement of HoN's income generation program, I received 35 vaccinated chickens, six weeks old, on a 100% subsidy. My poultry thrived, growing quickly and healthily, and by early November, they began laying eggs.
  >
  > I became the first successful entrepreneur in my community, earning NPR 8,400 by selling 420 eggs at NPR 20 per egg and an additional NPR 4,000 by selling two roosters at NPR 2,000 each. Besides generating income, I also provided my family with eggs for daily consumption.
  >
  > Feeling fortunate and empowered by my newfound knowledge and skills in professional poultry farming, I am deeply grateful to HEART of Nepal and CIAO KIDS for this golden opportunity. This venture has significantly improved my economic condition and will support my children's education. Thank you for your continued cooperation.

#### Story 2 — Laju Majhi's Journey to Educational Empowerment
- **Group:** "Paurakhi Mother's Group" of "Pariwartansheel Self Help Group"
- **Narrative:**
  > Ms. Laju Majhi, a 10-year-old resident of Ward No. 12 in Indrawati Rural Municipality, comes from a family of destitute and uneducated farmers. She lives with her father, Raju Majhi, and her mother, Kabita Majhi, in Dhotar village. Her father, a struggling farmer with limited land, faces prolonged unemployment, making it difficult to meet the family's basic needs, including food, warm clothing, medical treatment, and education.
  >
  > Due to these challenging circumstances, Laju was often irregular in her classes and frequently absent from school, spending much of her time helping her parents in the fields. Unfortunately, this led to her failing an exam. Recognizing her struggles, the HEART of Nepal (HoN) team began counselling her and her family on the importance of education. The field team provided full support, encouraging her to join tuition classes organized by HoN.
  >
  > With determination, Laju started attending both school and tuition classes. Her hard work paid off, and she showed significant improvement in the first terminal exam. She expressed heartfelt gratitude to the HoN team for their counselling, guidance, and practical support. Overjoyed with her success, she extends her thanks to the CK Team and HEART of Nepal for offering this life-changing opportunity.
